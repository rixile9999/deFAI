## 💬 Key Talking Points

### Technical Terms (Simplified Explanations)

1. **Hierarchical Federated Learning**
   > "A distributed system where data stays at the source,
   > and only encrypted statistics are shared upward."

2. **Homomorphic Encryption**
   > "A special encryption that allows mathematical operations
   > on encrypted data without revealing the actual values."

3. **Blind Aggregation**
   > "Combining encrypted statistics without being able to
   > see individual data points."

4. **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**
   > "A statistical method comparing treatment and control groups
   > before and after a policy intervention."

## ❓ Q&A Preparation

### Expected Questions & Answers

**Q: Is this real patient data?**

> **A**: "No, this demonstration uses simulated data generated from an Agent-Based Model
> to showcase the system's capabilities. The treatment/control assignment (Seoul vs. Gyeonggi)
> is purely for demonstration purposes. For real-world deployment, the system would use
> actual anonymized health records with proper regulatory approval."

**Q: How does this protect privacy?**

> **A**: "We use homomorphic encryption, which means data is encrypted
> at the source and stays encrypted during aggregation. Intermediate tiers
> can only perform mathematical operations without seeing actual values.
> Only the central government with the secret key can decrypt for final analysis."

**Q: Is this ready for real-world deployment?**

> **A**: "The technical infrastructure is production-ready, using battle-tested
> encryption libraries and federated learning frameworks. However, this demo uses
> simulated data. Real-world deployment would require integration with actual
> health information systems and regulatory approval."

**Q: What's the performance impact of encryption?**

> **A**: "Homomorphic encryption does add computational overhead, but our
> hierarchical approach distributes the workload. For policy analysis where
> we don't need real-time results, this trade-off is acceptable for the
> privacy benefits gained."

**Q: Can this work for other applications?**

> **A**: "Absolutely. The same architecture can be applied to any scenario
> requiring privacy-preserving analysis across organizational boundaries,
> such as finance, education, or environmental monitoring."

**Q: How accurate is the 71.2% figure?**

> **A**: "This result is statistically significant with a t-value of -6.61
> and p-value less than 0.001, meaning we can be 99.9% confident this effect
> is real and not due to chance."
